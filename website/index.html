The problem

The fertilizer aisle is a plastic aisle

Walk down any garden aisle. Plastic bottles of liquid. Plastic bags of granules. Plastic-coated beads that stay in the soil after the nutrients are gone. Feeding a plant should not work like this.

Measuring you can get wrong

Measuring cups, dosing caps, and ratios written for people managing acres. Get it wrong in a pot and you burn the roots. That fear is why so many plants never get fed at all.

Plastic that stays in your soil

Slow-release fertilizer works by wrapping every granule in a polymer coating. The nutrients leave. The coating does not. Researchers who sampled nineteen fields found coating fragments in every one.

Salts that shock, then vanish

Concentrated synthetic salts deliver a spike, then nothing, and feed the plant while doing nothing for the soil it lives in. They are also easy to over-apply, which is where salt crust and scorched tips come from.

One pod answers all three. Pre-measured so it cannot be over-applied, organic rather than synthetic salts, and wrapped in paper that dissolves into the soil. Read the full plastic story.

How it works

Three steps. No mess. No measuring.

1 Drop

Place a pod

Set one pre-measured BioPod at the base of your plant or just below the soil. The dose is already calibrated. There is nothing to scoop, weigh, or dilute.

2 Water

Water as normal

Water the way you always do. The cellulose shell dissolves in 30 to 90 seconds on contact with water and carries a balanced nutrient blend straight to the root zone.

3 Grow

Watch it grow

Nutrients release gradually as the pod breaks down over 8 to 10 weeks, feeding both your plant and the living soil around it. No residue, no leftover plastic, nothing to remove.

Why BioPod

Growth, protection, and balance at the root zone

Feeds where roots actually are

The pod sits at the root zone and releases there, instead of washing across the surface. Nutrients arrive where the plant takes them up, which is the difference between feeding a plant and feeding a pot.

Supports the plant's own defences

Beneficial Bacillus cultures wake in the soil and colonise the root zone. These are among the most studied helpful soil bacteria in plant research, documented to prime a plant's natural defences, what scientists call induced systemic resistance.

Balanced, never an excess

Four compartments release in stages: fast minerals, slow organic nitrogen, potassium, and microbes. Balance over weeks rather than a spike and a crash, with no synthetic salts to build up as crust.

Gentle around the household

Made from plant and ocean ingredients with no synthetic fertilizers, so it is family and pet friendly when used as directed. As with any plant food, store it away from pets and children.

One pod, one full feeding

Dosed by pot size, not by plant species, so there is nothing to guess. About one pod per six inches of pot. A 12-pack is twelve pods, roughly a dollar each. One pod per feeding for a small pot, two or three for a larger one.

Builds the soil, not just the plant

Microbes keep working after the nutrients are used, cycling what is already in the soil and building structure. This is a soil-health system, not a quick green-up, and it compounds season after season.

Inside the pod

A water-soluble shell and a living nutrient blend

BioPod starts with a cellulose shell made from FSC-sourced wood pulp fibers. It is the same plant fibre family as paper, fully biodegradable, and it dissolves in soil within 30 to 90 seconds when watered, leaving no residue.

Inside the shell is a balanced blend built on four natural pillars:

  • Plant proteins for slow-release nitrogen and steady, gentle growth
  • Sea kelp for trace minerals, natural growth compounds, and over 60 micronutrients
  • Mineral potassium for strong roots, blooms, and visible greening
  • Beneficial soil microbes that colonize the root zone, suppress root disease, and build long-term soil health

Each pillar is individually backed by published, peer-reviewed scientific literature. The combination and the multi-compartment delivery architecture are what make BioPod structurally different from anything else on the shelf.

The shell is not a plastic film, and it is not PVA, the synthetic polymer used in detergent pods. It is paper, and when it dissolves it leaves nutrients rather than fragments.
